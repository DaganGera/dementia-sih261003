Limitations

Hillpath is a prototype for screening support and memory activities. It is not a medical device and it does not diagnose.

Maturity

Implemented
Runs on real input. Five activities, reminders with the missed-dose rule, pairing and encrypted sharing without internet, the family view, the monthly check, and choosing the next activity level.
Simulated
Works, but only on synthetic people. The screening range, the trend and forecast, the sudden-change rule, and the twelve-month simulation. Screens say so.
Roadmap
Planned, not built. More languages with native speakers, voice answers, the health worker visit mode, a doctor report, clinical validation, and Android device testing.

Known limits, most important first

  1. No patient has used Hillpath. Results from play are personal trends or simulated.
  2. The screening range is trained on synthetic people only. No dataset that needs an application was used, so its accuracy for real people is unknown.
  3. The questions in the monthly check are Hillpath's own, not a validated instrument, and were designed for a tablet, not a face-to-face interview.
  4. The screens are in English only. Families can record messages in any language, but the words on screen are English until native speakers check other languages.
  5. Mood, sleep, illness, vision, hearing and years of schooling change scores. Hillpath asks about them, compares with people of similar schooling, and holds back a range when something suggests a cause other than dementia.
  6. Forecasts in the first weeks mostly show typical change. The stage outlook uses an assumed table, not data.
  7. The sudden-change rule from play data caught about three in four simulated episodes with about 0.3 false alarms per simulated person-year. The family checklist covers what it misses. Real performance is unknown.
  8. Reminders can be late on some phones unless exact alarms are allowed, and the Android app has not yet been installed on a phone.

What the activities do not do

They do not cure, stop or reverse dementia. They aim at engagement and wellbeing. NICE guideline NG97 recommends group cognitive stimulation therapy for mild to moderate dementia and advises against offering cognitive training to treat mild to moderate Alzheimer's disease, so Hillpath does not claim memory improvement.
