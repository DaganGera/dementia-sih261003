# Known gaps

Ranked by how much they matter to a person or a juror. Every item is something a reader could otherwise assume works.

1. **No real people.** Nobody has played Hillpath. Game-based results are personal trends or Simulated.
2. **English only.** The plan had 14 languages with tiers; the user chose English only with no native speakers. A signed language-pack manager exists in code (`packages/core/src/langpack.ts`: tiers T0 to T3, version-gated install, one-step rollback, `tools/content/build-pack.ts`, `tools/content/tier-check.ts`) so a real pack can be added later without a code change, but `TRUSTED_LANG_PUBLIC_KEY` is deliberately left empty: no downloaded pack can verify until a real translation and review pipeline runs and a publisher key is set. Family recordings work in any language, but every word on screen is English, and prompts spoken by the device use the device's English voice. Requirements R3, R4 and O3 are met only in structure.
3. **The screening model is trained on synthetic people.** No dataset needing an application was used. Its accuracy for real people is unknown. The stage outlook uses an assumed transition table. A monotone LightGBM and an Explainable Boosting Classifier were compared against the shipped ordinal model under a rule fixed before training (`ml/reports/m2-candidates.md`); the comparison itself still says nothing about real people, since the simulator flatters all three models the same way.
4. **The monthly check and the caregiver check-in are Hillpath's own.** The monthly check is not IQCODE, AD8 or any validated instrument, and has not been compared with a clinician's rating; the mood questions follow the PHQ-2 pattern. The caregiver check-in (`packages/core/src/burden.ts`) is six items written for this app, explicitly labelled not a validated scale, and its answers never leave the carer's own device: they are not synced, not in the doctor report, and only ever soften how many non-urgent alerts that one device receives.
5. **Nothing has run on an Android device.** Native alarms, camera scanning, microphone recording and storage inside the Android WebView are untested. Android Studio and JDK 21 are not installed (`docs/android.md`).
6. **QR and relay sync trust the circle key.** A share is encrypted with the circle key and signed, but if the sender is not in the paired list the signature is checked against a key inside the message, so it proves integrity, not identity. Any device holding the circle key can write any record. The plan's rule that only a device with reminder rights may change reminders is not enforced. The relay only ever stores ciphertext and a bearer hash, but it is not deployed anywhere public; sharing has been tested against its in-memory and local Node store, not a live Cloudflare Worker.
7. **At-rest protection is browser-level, and the tablet's own lock is light.** Ops, keys and recordings are sealed with a non-extractable key held in IndexedDB. A rooted device or a person with the unlocked phone can still use the app. The person's tablet can be given a PIN (salted PBKDF2, slower retries after five misses) or a platform passkey, but this is meant to keep out an accidental tap, not to resist someone who has the device for a long time; there is still no lock on the family or caregiver phone itself.
8. **Recordings do not sync.** Family voice recordings and full-size photos stay on the device where they were made. Small thumbnails and short postcard voice clips (kept under about 15 seconds) sync inside records; longer family songs do not.
9. **The sudden-change rule from play data is modest, and got a little worse.** In simulation it now catches about 15 of 30 episodes (was 22 of 30 when it was first tuned, against a five-activity roster; the current build has ten activities, which spreads play thinner per domain). The family checklist is the main path. Play must be frequent for the data rule to work. Not retuned in this pass; tracked here rather than quietly lowering the bar elsewhere.
10. **Legal review.** The design follows the ideas of the DPDP Act 2023 and Rule 11 on guardians, but it has had no legal review and must not be used with real patient records.
11. **The hero footage has unconfirmed rights** (`docs/landing-media.md`).
12. **Wording and colour have not been checked by a person with dementia, a caregiver or a clinician.** The elder-simulation pass and TalkBack pass are still to do.
13. **FHIR is structural only.** The doctor report's FHIR Bundle passes Hillpath's own structural check (a Composition first, referenced resources present); it has not been run through an HL7 validator (this machine's Java is 1.8, too old for the usual validator tooling), and no ABDM profile is claimed.
14. **The orientation board's festival dates need yearly upkeep.** A movable (lunar) festival's date changes every year; the board just repeats whatever month and day the family typed in, so a family that does not update it will eventually show the wrong date for that festival. Fixed-date festivals are unaffected.
